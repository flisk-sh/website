Cost is only the first reason.

Saving money gets attention. These nine reasons are why teams stay. Each one removes a weekly annoyance your developers stopped complaining about.

Day-one onboarding

Clone the repo, run one command, get the whole system. New hires ship code before lunch instead of configuring machines for a fortnight.

DevOps stays on production

Local environments stop eating infrastructure time. Your platform people work on uptime and delivery, not on someone's broken machine setup.

Runs on ordinary machines

Proven daily on a {{ site.data.flisk.machine }}. No cloud computers to rent, no GPU bills, and it works on planes and bad hotel wifi.

Secrets without spreadsheets

Every service gets its own managed identity and credentials through Infisical. Nothing pasted into chat, nothing committed by accident.

Documents that cannot rot

Architecture diagrams and assistant briefings regenerate from the live system on every boot. The docs always match reality.

Same stack on every machine

Ten developers, ten identical environments. "Works on my machine" stops being a sentence anyone says out loud.

Fast feedback loop

14 tiny services healthy in 4.6s on a 16 GB M1 after images existed. Not a cold boot. Hot reload while you type and selective startup for the services your task touches. Claim details.

AI assistants understand it

A generated briefing tells coding assistants how the stack fits together, so AI help is correct on the first answer instead of the fifth.

No lock-in, ever

Everything generated is standard Docker and TypeScript tooling under an MIT license. If you outgrow Flisk, you keep every file.
